# Privacy Regulation Quick Reference (summary for internal guidance, not legal advice)

## GDPR (European Union)
Personal data is any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Special categories
include health, biometric, genetic, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs and sexual
orientation. Principles relevant to redaction: data minimisation (share only what is necessary), purpose
limitation, storage limitation, and integrity and confidentiality. Pseudonymised data is still personal data;
properly anonymised data is not. Serious infringements can attract fines of up to 20 million euros or 4% of
worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

## India - Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP)
Applies to digital personal data processed in India and to processing outside India for offering goods or
services to people in India. Data fiduciaries must implement reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal
data breaches and must notify the Data Protection Board and affected data principals of breaches. Penalties
for failing to take reasonable security safeguards can reach 250 crore rupees. Aadhaar numbers additionally
fall under the Aadhaar Act, which restricts their display and sharing; masking all but the last four digits or
full redaction is expected when Aadhaar appears in documents.

## CCPA / CPRA (California, USA)
Personal information includes identifiers such as name, postal address, email, SSN, driver's licence and
passport numbers. Sensitive personal information includes SSN, financial account details, precise geolocation
and health data. Consumers have rights to know, delete, correct and limit use of sensitive personal information.

## HIPAA (USA, health data)
Protected health information can be de-identified by removing the 18 Safe Harbor identifiers, which include
names, geographic subdivisions smaller than a state, dates related to an individual (except year), phone and fax
numbers, email addresses, SSNs, medical record numbers, account numbers, licence numbers, device identifiers,
URLs, IP addresses, biometric identifiers and full-face photographs.

## Practical implications for redaction
Redact before sharing, not after. Keep an audit trail that proves what was removed without retaining the
removed values. Prefer black-out for external disclosure and labelled pseudonymisation for internal analysis.
Re-verify outputs, because incomplete redaction (for example text hidden under a drawn box in a PDF) is itself
a reportable breach in many jurisdictions.
